What this tool does

It reads the documents that smart money reads — SEC filings — and surfaces what matters.

What "smart money" actually does: They don't watch stock prices. They read filings. Every US public company files documents with the SEC. Those documents tell you what hedge funds bought (13F), what activists are doing (13D), what insiders are trading (Form 4), and what events happened that the company must disclose (8-K).

Why this is free: The SEC's EDGAR system is funded by taxpayers and is required by law to be publicly accessible. Bloomberg charges $24,000 a year to wrap a fancy UI around this data. We just go to the source.

How to use the three modes:

  • Watchlist: Add stocks you care about. See every filing they make, with critical events flagged.
  • Theme search: Search the text inside all filings for keywords.
  • Live firehose: See every filing of a specific type as it happens.

About CORS proxies: The SEC's data API blocks browser-direct access for performance reasons. We route requests through a free CORS proxy. If one proxy is slow, the dropdown at the top lets you switch.

Filing type cheat sheet

8-K — Material event. Required within 4 business days when something important happens. Always read these.

SC 13D — A new investor accumulated more than 5% with intent to influence. Often activists. Loud bullish signal.

SC 13G — Same 5% threshold but passive. Less loud but worth noting.

Form 4 — Insider bought or sold their own stock. Buys with own money are the cleanest bullish signal in finance.

13F-HR — Institutional manager's quarterly holdings list. 45-day delayed.

10-Q / 10-K — Quarterly / annual financial reports.

S-3 / 424B — Company selling new shares. Usually dilutive.

DEF 14A — Proxy statement before annual meeting.
